# adaptmem v0.6 + v0.7 — Fork Integration Plan

*Companion to [`adaptmem-orthogonal-layers.md`](adaptmem-orthogonal-layers.md). That earlier note framed adaptmem as a layer below retrieval — a swappable encoder. Two releases have shipped since (v0.6.0 on 2026-05-14 production-hardened the daemon; v0.7.0 on 2026-05-23 raised the training ceiling). This note catalogs what is new and proposes concrete experiments for the techempower-org fork.*

## What changed upstream

### v0.6.0 (2026-05-14) — production daemon + bench coupling

The lift that matters for us:

- **Matched-protocol harness** (`benchmarks/mempal_bench_with_ft.py`). adaptmem monkey-patches `benchmarks/longmemeval_bench.py:101` `_bench_embed_fn` with an FT encoder and runs the measurement end-to-end through our own scorer — zero shim-shape divergence. The hook is already in our fork; we did not have to negotiate an integration surface.
- **Cross-harness probe** (`benchmarks/jphein_chunk_x_encoder.py`). They reuse our `benchmarks/chunk_strategy_ablation.py` with an FT-encoder swap. The encoder axis is now measurable inside our chunking-axis harness, which is the cleanest way to test whether per [#390](https://github.com/MemPalace/mempalace/pull/390) chunk_size remains the dominant lever once the encoder is also tuned.
- **CodeSearchNet bench track**. FT-Code-5000 hits R@1=0.926 / MRR=0.952 on python source (off-the-shelf MiniLM is 0.6477 / 0.7406). This is the second-domain validation the FT recipe needed — it is not LongMemEval-specific overfitting.
- **SQLite-backed `CorpusStore`** (the daemon survives restarts without re-encoding). Conceptually parallel to our palace-daemon's persistent posture; not directly integrable but worth referencing if we ever spin up a benchmark-isolated adaptmem-served palace.
- Helm chart + raw k8s manifests + Koyeb config — operationally interesting but not on our hot path.

### v0.7.0 (2026-05-23) — training-recipe upgrade

- **`n_negatives` (K hard negatives per query)** multiplies training signal per labelled example. The "300 labelled queries → R@5=0.995" ceiling came in part from this. Same training data, more negatives sampled, larger effective batch.
- **Gradient accumulation** for low-VRAM training. Lets the 2080ti participate without spilling. Familiar's P102 was already capable.
- **`CachedMultipleNegativesRankingLoss`** as an alternative loss; trade memory for compute.
- **Default reranker upgraded to BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3** (from `ms-marco-MiniLM-L-12-v2`). Heavier, slower, better.
- **Eval reports R@1 misses with `question_type` breakdown** — exposes which LongMemEval categories the encoder still loses. Matches our PR #27 (entities-only n=50 cat_6 baseline) framing: we already think category-resolution.

## Where our fork sits today

| Layer | Current | Adaptmem-shaped alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Encoder | `nomic-embed-text-v1.5.gguf` via llama-server (familiar P102, port 11435) | `minilm-lme-ft-300` (90 MB SentenceTransformer) or our-corpus-FT variant |
| Reranker | cross-encoder via palace-daemon, gated by `PALACE_RERANK_ENABLED=true` | `BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3` (heavier, +R@1 lift in v0.7 measurements) |
| Substrate | postgres + pgvector + AGE on familiar:5433 (370K+ drawers) | unchanged — encoder swap is search-time, ingest-time, or both |
| Bench harness | `benchmarks/longmemeval_bench.py` with `_bench_embed_fn` hook | already shaped for monkey-patching; that is exactly what v0.6's matched-protocol harness uses |

Three integration surfaces, two of them already wired:

1. **Bench-only encoder swap.** Run their `mempal_bench_with_ft.py` against our fork's bench, FT-300 in slot. Measures isolated encoder lift — no daemon changes, no migration risk. *Fastest first reading.*
2. **Daemon-served FT encoder.** Replace nomic-embed-text in palace-daemon's embedding pipeline with FT-300 served either as a SentenceTransformer in-process or behind a sidecar. Measures production-shaped lift. *Real product move.*
3. **Our-corpus FT.** Train an FT model on a labelled subset of OUR drawers (not LongMemEval). Measures domain-tuned encoder against actual production traffic. *The interesting long-tail bet.*

## Concrete experiments

These are the next-N-thing experiments, ordered by ROI vs cost.

### E1 — bench-only FT-300 swap (apples-to-apples upstream comparison)
- Pull adaptmem's published `minilm-lme-ft-300` artifact.
- Run `benchmarks/longmemeval_bench.py` with `--embed-model minilm-lme-ft-300` (matched-protocol).
- Expected: R@5=0.990 raw, R@5≈0.998 with hybrid_v4. Confirms the published number reproduces on our infra.
- **Cost:** 1 GPU-hour, no code changes beyond `_bench_embed_fn` wiring.
- **Blocks on:** nothing. Can run pre-relation-triples.

### E2 — chunk_size × encoder ablation re-run
- Re-run `chunk_strategy_ablation.py` once with `nomic-embed-text` and once with `minilm-lme-ft-300`. Hold chunk_size axis at the [#390](https://github.com/MemPalace/mempalace/pull/390) settings.
- Tests whether the chunk_size lever stays dominant once the encoder is also better-tuned, or whether the two axes are partially redundant.
- **Cost:** half a day of bench-runner time.
- **Blocks on:** E1 working end-to-end.

### E3 — palace-daemon embedding-pipeline swap (production-shaped)
- Plumb `minilm-lme-ft-300` into palace-daemon as either an in-process SentenceTransformer or a llama-server-compatible sidecar.
- Re-run the entities-only baseline from SME PR #27 (`familiar.jphe.in:8085`, 50q cat_6, `--skip-judge`).
- Tests whether the bench-only lift carries through the daemon's full search path (BM25 + vector + graph fusion + rerank).
- **Cost:** 1-2 days. The daemon's embedding hook is the unknown; may need a small palace-daemon PR.
- **Blocks on:** E1 numbers being in the expected range. If they are not, the daemon swap will not save it.

### E4 — relation-triples-aware re-baseline
- Once the relation-triples backfill completes (current ETA ~2 days at 100/min, 276K queue depth), re-run SME PR #27's protocol with both encoders in slot.
- Produces the "with triples + with FT-300" reading that closes the gap to the upstream-reported numbers on our substrate.
- **Cost:** automatable; reuse the SME PR #27 harness verbatim.
- **Blocks on:** relation-triples backfill, E3.

### E5 — our-corpus FT (long-tail, real-traffic measurement)
- Build a labelled subset from our drawers: ~300 query→drawer pairs from production search logs or from JP's chat history (filtered for clear gold).
- Run adaptmem's v0.7 training recipe with `n_negatives` and `CachedMNRL` for ~1 epoch on katana 2080ti.
- Evaluate against held-out queries from the same source. Measures whether domain-tuning to OUR corpus beats LongMemEval-FT on OUR queries.
- **Cost:** 2-3 days, mostly labelling. Training is ~1 GPU-hour.
- **Blocks on:** labelling protocol — what is "gold" for a drawer-retrieval query is fuzzier than LongMemEval's needle-in-haystack canon.

## Decision tree

```
Has E1 reproduced the published number?
├── yes → run E2 (chunk-axis interaction), then E3 (daemon swap)
└── no  → file an issue with nakata-app; cross-harness divergence is itself a finding

After E3:
Did daemon-served FT-300 lift the SME PR #27 baseline meaningfully?
├── yes → ship the encoder swap, hold E5 for v3.4 cycle
└── no  → root-cause whether rerank stage is dominating (rerank-off A/B), then decide

After E4:
Closes the gap to upstream's R@5 with triples present?
├── yes → publish the reading in `docs/research/2026-05-24-memory-system-benchmarks.md` Appendix A
└── no  → triples are not the differentiator we hoped; reframe the research paper accordingly
```

## What this does *not* do

- Does not address E2E QA scoring (LongMemEval issue #19's full goal). That is upstream-blocked on the SME `mempalace-daemon` adapter's haystack-ingest topology.
- Does not commit to upstreaming an adaptmem-aware path into MemPalace's main bench harness. Our fork is the right place to prove the integration; upstream PR comes later.
- Does not propose retraining the cross-encoder reranker. v0.7's bge-reranker-v2-m3 upgrade is upstream's choice; we should A/B test before adopting.

## Open questions

1. **Does adaptmem's FT-300 generalise off-LongMemEval?** Their CodeSearchNet result says yes (FT recipe survives domain shift). E5 tests this on *our* domain specifically.
2. **Where in palace-daemon does the embedding-pipeline live?** This determines whether E3 is a 50-line PR or a 500-line PR. (TODO: code-read pass before scheduling E3.)
3. **Does the chunk_size × encoder interaction matter for hybrid_v4?** E2 measures this directly. If chunk_size dominates regardless of encoder, the encoder swap is incremental; if they interact, both need re-tuning.
